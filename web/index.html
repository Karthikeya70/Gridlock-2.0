TARGETED ENFORCEMENT

Enforcement Priority

220 m cells ranked by how much traffic flow they cost, how chronic they are, and whether they sit on a junction

The city is split into ~220 m squares ("blocks"). Each block gets a priority score from 0 to 100, based mostly on how much traffic harm its illegal parking causes, plus how many days it happens, whether it sits on a junction, and whether its neighbours are also bad.

  • P1: the top 25 blocks. Patrol every day.
  • P2: the next 75. Patrol regularly.
  • P3: other blocks that a statistical test says are real hotspots (99% sure), not random bad luck.

Each block also has a patrol window: the 3 hours when parking there hurts traffic most. Click a row to fly to it on the map.

Glossary

Every term used in this app, in plain words.

Ticket / e-challanA digital parking fine issued by Bengaluru Traffic Police (BTP), with GPS location, time, vehicle and offence.
Impact pointOur unit of traffic harm. 1 point = one car parked at the kerb during rush hour, not at a junction.
PCUPassenger Car Unit: how much road space a vehicle takes compared to a car. Car = 1, scooter = 0.5, bus = 2.2. From the Indian Roads Congress standard IRC:106.
Obstruction weightHow much moving lane an offence blocks. Parking at the kerb = 1, on a main road = 2, double parking (a second row) = 3.
JunctionA named BTP traffic junction. Parking there counts 1.5× because junctions are where traffic already queues.
Block / cellA ~220 m × 220 m square of the city, small enough for one patrol to cover.
Hotspot test (Gi*)A standard statistical test (Getis-Ord Gi*) that checks whether a block and its neighbours are unusually bad, so we don't chase one-off spikes.
Priority score (EPI)Enforcement Priority Index, 0–100. Blends traffic harm, how many days it happens, junction share and hotspot strength.
P1 / P2 / P3Priority tiers: top 25 blocks, next 75, then the other statistically real hotspots.
Patrol windowThe 3-hour slot when a block's parking harms traffic most, i.e. when to send officers.
ForecastA prediction of tickets and traffic harm per station, per hour, for a chosen date, learned from past patterns.
MAEMean Absolute Error: on average, how far the prediction is from reality (here, in tickets per station per hour). Lower is better.
Top-10 hit rateOut of each day's 10 busiest stations, how many the forecast named correctly.
Data leakageWhen a model accidentally sees the answers during training, so its test score looks better than it really is. Fixed here.

Cleaning

Congestion impact score

The dataset has no speed or volume feed, so impact is estimated from what each ticket tells us about the road space it removed. Every violation gets:

impact = PCU(vehicle) × obstruction(offence) × junction × demand(hour)
  • PCU is the Passenger Car Unit from IRC:106-1990. A bus parked on the carriageway takes 2.2× the space of a car, a scooter 0.5×.
  • Obstruction is how much moving lane the offence takes. Kerbside no-parking = 1.0, main road = 2.0, near a crossing or signal = 2.0, double parking = 3.0.
  • Junction = 1.5 when the ticket is tagged to a BTP junction, because intersections are capacity bottlenecks.
  • Demand is relative traffic demand by hour (twin peaks), so a blocked lane at 9 AM costs more than one at 3 AM.

Hotspot detection

The original DBSCAN run (150 m, 60k-point sample) chained central Bengaluru into one mega-cluster of 12k points, which is too big to patrol. Instead the full dataset is binned into ~220 m cells, a block a patrol can actually cover:

  • Getis-Ord Gi* on impact (3×3 neighbourhood) flags cells whose surroundings are significantly hotter than the city (z > 2.58).
  • EPI = 55% impact rank + 25% persistence (share of days active) + 10% junction share + 10% Gi* rank.
  • Each cell reports its dominant road, junction, vehicle, offence, and the 3-hour window with most impact, which becomes the recommended patrol slot.
